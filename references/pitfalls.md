# High-Signal Pitfalls

Use this reference during final review and update it only with recurring, generalisable failures.

## Drafting before facts are stable

**Failure:** fluent prose hides missing ownership, motives, or results.

**Correction:** create an evidence map first; leave uncertain items unresolved instead of completing them plausibly.

## Asking for every technical detail

**Failure:** the intake becomes a project defence and obscures the one decision that reveals the applicant's thinking.

**Correction:** apply the detail value test and stop when additional information will not enter the statement.

## Expanding the CV

**Failure:** each experience gets background, tasks, and results, but the statement has no argument.

**Correction:** choose a core experience and add another only if it changes the reader's conclusion.

## Prestige bias

**Failure:** a famous institution, competition, technology, or professor is included despite weak personal contribution.

**Correction:** rank by relevance, agency, evidence, and narrative value.

## Ownership inflation

**Failure:** team methods, results, or awards become first-person achievements.

**Correction:** audit every claim as `I`, `we`, or `project`.

## Metric drift

**Failure:** percentages, components, datasets, or baselines are blurred into a stronger claim.

**Correction:** preserve the metric's exact scope and state what was not measured.

## Generic reflection

**Failure:** the applicant claims growth without naming a changed method, understanding, or question.

**Correction:** tie reflection to a specific decision or result, or delete it.

## Literature name-dropping

**Failure:** the statement summarises a paper but does not show what it changed for the applicant.

**Correction:** keep one mechanism or idea and connect it to an existing question.

## Generic or stale programme fit

**Failure:** praise could apply to any university, or a current catalogue is presented as a guaranteed future offering.

**Correction:** verify official sources and connect each resource to a demonstrated need using time-aware language.

## Overexposing limitations

**Failure:** an honest statement becomes a list of everything the project failed to achieve.

**Correction:** include only the boundary that motivates the next stage of learning.

## Forced structure

**Failure:** five paragraphs, a fixed word count, or a research opening is imposed regardless of the prompt and evidence.

**Correction:** let requirements and narrative function determine structure.

## Whole-document humanising

**Failure:** a full rewrite makes sentence rhythm uniform, weakens ownership, or changes claims while chasing an AI-detector score.

**Correction:** audit first and make local edits; never promise a detector result.
