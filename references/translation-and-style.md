# Translation and Style Review

Use this reference for translation, sentence-level editing, tone, word limits, and final language checks.

## Translate the argument, not the syntax

Preserve facts, causal relationships, emphasis, and degree of certainty. Rebuild sentences for natural target-language prose rather than following source-language order.

Do not add sophistication by introducing motives, interpretations, or technical claims absent from the source.

## Match the applicant's plausible voice

Use precise terminology for academic and technical content. Keep surrounding prose direct. Avoid making every sentence maximally polished, symmetrical, or abstract.

If the applicant provides a genuine writing sample, use it to calibrate sentence length, vocabulary, and connective habits. Do not imitate errors that reduce clarity.

## Local style audit

Check for:

- generic openings and conclusions;
- repeated `this experience taught me` summaries;
- abstract-noun clusters;
- inflated significance or promotional language;
- consecutive sentences with identical rhythm;
- excessive transition words;
- negative parallelisms such as repeated `not only ... but also ...`;
- sentences over roughly 30 words with several subordinate clauses;
- spelling-system inconsistency;
- terms whose translation changes the factual claim.

Change only the sentences that need it. Do not rewrite the entire statement merely to vary wording.

## Optional external humanizer

Use a specialised humanizer only when the user explicitly requests a style or AI-tell audit and a compatible skill is available. Use audit-first mode after facts and structure are stable.

Do not allow an external style pass to change facts, numbers, programme names, citations, contribution ownership, or uncertainty. Prefer targeted edits to a full-document rewrite. If no compatible skill is available, use the checklist above.

Do not claim that editing will lower an AI-detector score. Detection tools are unstable; optimise for truthful, natural, applicant-owned writing.

## Word count and formatting

Follow the institution's definition and limit where stated. Otherwise report the counting method used. Recheck the count after translation because source and target languages do not expand predictably.

Use a consistent spelling convention appropriate to the applicant and destination. Do not treat British and American variants as errors when used consistently.
