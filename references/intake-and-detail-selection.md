# Intake and Detail Selection

Use this reference when interviewing an applicant or deciding whether the supplied evidence is sufficient.

## Minimum evidence card

For each candidate experience, establish only what is needed to explain:

- the problem or question;
- the applicant's own action;
- why that action was chosen;
- the result or evidence;
- the insight, change in understanding, or next question;
- its relevance to the proposed study, if it will be used for fit.

Missing answers are gaps, not invitations to infer. Do not demand equal detail for every field if the experience will only play a supporting role.

## Select depth, not volume

Do not investigate every part of an experience equally. Identify one **depth anchor**: a consequential decision, unexpected result, failure, trade-off, or observation that best reveals how the applicant thinks.

Develop that anchor through as many of these links as the evidence supports:

`expectation -> evidence -> reasoning -> decision -> consequence -> next question`

The chain is a diagnostic tool, not a mandatory sentence template.

## Detail value test

Ask for or retain an implementation detail only when it does at least one of the following:

1. explains why the problem was difficult;
2. proves individual contribution;
3. explains a consequential choice;
4. supplies credible evidence for a result;
5. reveals a change in reasoning;
6. connects the experience to a concrete need for further study.

Otherwise classify it as CV-only, interview-only, supporting documentation, or unnecessary.

Hardware models, dependency versions, complete metric tables, and full module inventories usually fail this test. Keep them only when they are central to the challenge or decision.

## Question strategy

1. Begin with broad questions about problem, role, action, result, and learning.
2. Identify the highest-leverage missing link.
3. Ask a small number of targeted follow-ups about that link.
4. Stop when further detail would not change an admissions reader's view of the applicant's ability, motivation, or fit.

Do not interrogate for completeness. The goal is a credible narrative, not a forensic reconstruction of the project.

## Stop conditions

Stop questioning an experience when:

- personal contribution is distinguishable from team work;
- the key action has a reason;
- at least one result or observable consequence is available;
- the reflection follows from the event rather than being generic;
- remaining details would be unlikely to enter the statement.

If a missing fact affects truthfulness or changes the story, pause and ask. If it only adds texture, proceed without it.
