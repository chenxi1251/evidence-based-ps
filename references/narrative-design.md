# Narrative Design

Use this reference for story lines, paragraph plans, and drafting.

## Start from a question, not a slogan

Derive the central academic interest from a real observation, difficulty, contradiction, or decision. Avoid childhood destiny, broad claims about technological change, and unsupported statements of passion.

The opening should let the reader understand:

- what problem interests the applicant;
- where that interest came from;
- why the rest of the statement is being told.

It need not be dramatic.

## Build causal movement

For the main experience, preserve the relationship between:

`problem -> applicant action -> reason -> evidence -> interpretation -> next question`

Do not merely expand CV bullets. Technical details should reveal reasoning, not demonstrate vocabulary.

## Use reflection carefully

Reflection must name a specific change in method, understanding, or question. Delete conclusions that could follow from almost any project, such as learning that teamwork matters or that theory and practice are both important, unless the experience supplies a distinctive reason.

Do not list every project limitation. Include a boundary only when it explains the need for further study or the next research question.

## Integrate literature

Mention a paper, theory, or researcher only when the applicant genuinely engaged with it and can explain one relevant mechanism or idea. Connect it to the applicant's existing question. Do not summarise a paper for prestige or claim to have performed the paper's research.

## Connect programme fit

Why School should answer:

`established need -> specific resource -> intended use`

It should respond to the preceding experiences rather than appear as an isolated catalogue.

## Structure flexibly

Do not force a fixed paragraph count. Each paragraph should have one primary job, and each new experience should add a new conclusion.

After outlining, run a duplication test:

- Does more than one paragraph prove the same quality?
- Does the ending merely repeat the opening?
- Does Why School repeat the career goal without adding a resource or plan?
- Does a literature paragraph repeat the project's lesson rather than extend it?

Revise the plan before polishing sentences.
