# Experience Ranking

Use this reference to decide what belongs in the statement.

## Rank by function

Classify each experience as:

- **Core evidence:** directly explains academic interest, relevant ability, or readiness; contains a defensible personal contribution and a useful depth anchor.
- **Supporting evidence:** adds a different capability, perspective, or progression not already proved by the core experience.
- **CV-only:** credible and useful, but too routine, weakly connected, duplicative, or too thin for narrative treatment.
- **Do not use:** ownership is unclear, claims cannot be verified, or inclusion would mislead.

## Selection criteria

Prefer experiences that provide several of the following:

- direct relevance to the target field;
- clear personal agency;
- a decision supported by evidence;
- an outcome that can be stated precisely;
- a change in understanding;
- a natural bridge to further study.

Do not prefer an experience merely because the organisation, competition, university, or technology sounds prestigious.

## Marginal-value test

Before adding a secondary experience, ask:

- What new conclusion should the reader draw from it?
- Is that conclusion already established by the main experience?
- Can it be supported without inventing or overstating personal contribution?
- Is it worth the words it consumes?

If it only repeats technical interest, teamwork, leadership, or problem-solving, omit it or leave it in the CV.

## Coverage, not accumulation

A strong statement often uses one experience in depth and one or two compact supporting references. This is a common pattern, not a fixed structure. Choose the smallest set of experiences that proves the intended claims.
